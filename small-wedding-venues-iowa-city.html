Small Wedding Venues in Iowa City

Cozy, personal spaces for micro-weddings, elopements, and intimate dinners with the people who matter most, from 10 to 60 guests.

Why More Couples Are Going Small

About one in three couples Maren worked with last year invited fewer than 50 guests. A smaller wedding usually means a lower total cost, more time to talk with each guest, and the chance to spend more on the details you care about, like a great dinner or a talented photographer. It also opens up beautiful spaces that simply cannot hold a 150-person reception.

Our Favorite Small Venues

The Juniper Room

Above a downtown bistro · Up to 40 seated

$500 room fee, waived with a $2,000 food minimum

A private dining room with exposed brick, candlelit tables, and a window view of the pedestrian mall. Dinner comes straight from the kitchen downstairs.

Best for: a ceremony-and-dinner wedding where food is the star.

Tallgrass Garden Courtyard

East side neighborhood · Up to 30 guests

$350 for a four-hour block

A walled garden with native prairie plantings, a stone path, and a small arbor for the ceremony. Ten folding chairs and two serving tables are included.

Best for: spring and summer ceremonies followed by dessert and toasts.

Riverstone Chapel

Near the river, west Iowa City · Up to 60 guests

$450 for a two-hour ceremony slot

A restored 1890s limestone chapel with stained glass and original wooden pews. It is non-denominational, and you may bring your own officiant.

Best for: a traditional ceremony before a reception somewhere else.

The Reading Room at Brickstone Inn

Historic bed and breakfast · Up to 25 guests

$800, including one night in the inn's suite

A wood-paneled library with a fireplace, leather armchairs, and floor-to-ceiling bookshelves. The innkeepers can arrange coffee, cake, and champagne.

Best for: fall and winter elopements with a warm, storybook feel.

Maple Street Gallery

Northside · Up to 45 guests

$600 evening rental

A small white-walled gallery with track lighting and a sidewalk patio. You can bring your own caterer and drinks with a licensed server.

Best for: a modern, relaxed cocktail-style reception.

Tips for Planning a Small Wedding

  • Write your guest list first, then pick the venue. With small spaces, five extra guests can mean you no longer fit.
  • Ask about vendor minimums. Some caterers and photographers charge the same minimum fee no matter how few guests you have, so look for small-wedding packages.
  • Pair a ceremony site with a dinner spot. Riverstone Chapel followed by dinner at The Juniper Room is one of our favorite combinations, and the two are a short walk apart.
  • Send personal invitations. A smaller list means you can write a note on each card, which guests remember.
  • Be clear and kind about the guest count. A simple line such as "We are celebrating with immediate family" helps avoid hurt feelings.
Sample micro-wedding budget from Priya: A 30-guest Saturday wedding at Tallgrass Garden Courtyard with a catered dinner can come in around $6,200: $350 venue, $1,650 catering, $1,800 photography (four hours), $600 flowers, $250 cake, $550 rentals, and $1,000 for attire and extras.
